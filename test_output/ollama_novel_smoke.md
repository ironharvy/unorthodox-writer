# The Keeper's Signal


## Chapter 1: The First Whisper

The light had been steady for weeks, a cold white beam slicing through the mist like a blade. I checked the bulb, then the lens, then the wiring. Nothing had changed. Yet, as I stood on the platform, the beam flickered—once, twice, then a third time, slow and deliberate. A pattern. I frowned, my fingers curling around the railing. The sea was restless, but this wasn’t the sea. It was something else.  

Mara came at dusk, his boots squelching in the mud. “You see it, don’t you?” he said, his voice rough as sand. “The hunger.” I didn’t answer. He leaned against the doorframe, watching me. “It’s not just the water out there. It’s the dark. It’s waiting.”  

I turned back to the light. It pulsed once more, and I felt it in my chest, a quiet, insistent throb.


## Chapter 2: The Watcher's Call

The light kept changing. I marked the intervals on a scrap of paper, the numbers growing stranger, the rhythm more urgent. Each pulse felt like a rhythm that hummed in my ribs, a vibration that didn’t belong to the world I knew. I told myself it was the wind, the shifting of the lens, the crack in the bulb. But the pattern held. It was a language. I could feel it in my bones, the way the beam stretched across the dark, carving out a path I didn’t understand.  

Mara came again at dawn, his coat soaked through. “You’re chasing something you can’t stop,” he said, his voice low, like a warning. “It’s not a signal. It’s a door.” I didn’t answer. I was too busy listening.  

Then it came—the whisper, soft as a sigh, curling around the edges of the beam. It was her voice. Not the memory, not the ghost, but her. *“Eli.”* I turned, but she wasn’t there. Only the light, growing brighter, hotter, until my hands trembled as I turned it toward the sea.


## Chapter 3: The Last Light

The beam had grown too bright, too wide, a wound in the sky. I stood at the edge of the platform, the salt air thick with the taste of something ancient. The whisper was gone, but the hunger remained, pressing against the walls of the tower like a tide without a shore. I reached for the switch, my fingers trembling, and pulled it. The light dimmed, then died.  

A silence fell, heavy as a storm. The sea didn’t move. The wind didn’t stir. I waited, expecting the dark to swallow me whole, but it didn’t. The beam flickered once, then snuffed out.  

Mara stood at the base of the stairs, his face pale, his hands clenched. “It’s not over,” he said. His voice was steady, but there was no warning in it. No hope.  

I didn’t answer. The light was gone. The Watcher had not come. But the sea was still, and the dark was quiet. And in that silence, I knew the truth could not be undone.
