# nemotron-3-nano:4b

Rain slicked the porch steps as she stepped inside, the scent of wet earth and old pine rising from the floorboards. Her fingers brushed the chipped blue mug on the counter—her father’s, left there years ago. Outside, the orchard she’d known since childhood was gone. In its place, a thicket of black hawthorn, its thorns catching the weak afternoon light, swallowed the land. The apple tree, her father’s pride, had split open, its gnarled trunk now a knot of gnarled branches. No apples. No fruit. Just the hollow echo of what was there.

She moved slowly, the silence pressing against her eardrums. A crow landed on the dead branch, pecked at nothing, then flew off into the canopy. Her breath caught. Not a sound. Just the wind, rustling leaves that weren’t hers. She reached for the handle of the kitchen door, then stopped. The house hadn’t changed. The walls, the windows, the same cracked linoleum. But the world beyond them had. The orchard was a forest. Not a forest she knew. A forest that didn’t belong to her father’s hands. She touched the bark of a hawthorn, cold and smooth. A memory, sharp and sweet, of his calloused fingers pruning, humming off-key. Now, only the quiet remained. She turned back to the kitchen, the mug still in her hand. The forest waited. She didn’t enter it. The house held its breath.
