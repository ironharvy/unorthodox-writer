She hadn’t meant to walk past the gate. The path was still there, worn smooth by decades of her father’s boots, but the orchard had swallowed it whole. She stopped at the edge of the clearing, her hand hovering over the rusted gate latch. The air smelled different—damp, like the inside of a refrigerator, and something else, something sweet and rotting. She stepped over the threshold.

The trees were wrong. Not just taller, not just older. They had grown into something else, their trunks thick and gnarled, their leaves a shade too green. The orchard had become a forest, and the fruit trees were gone, replaced by something that had not been there before. She crouched to touch the earth, her fingers brushing moss and roots that twisted like veins beneath the soil. A small, brittle branch snapped under her palm.

She walked deeper, past the place where the old apple trees had stood, past the swing that had broken long ago. The air was heavy, and the silence was not the kind of silence that comes with absence. It was the silence of something watching, something waiting. She found the well, its stone rim cracked, the water black and still. She knelt, her reflection staring back, and for a moment, she thought she saw her father’s face in the water—old, tired, and smiling.

She stood, her breath shallow, and turned back toward the road. The forest had grown in the night, she thought, or maybe it had always been there, and she had just never looked. She walked away, the gate closing behind her with a soft, final sigh.
